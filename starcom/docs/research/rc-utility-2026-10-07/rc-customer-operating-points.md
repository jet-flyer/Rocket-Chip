## 1. Altitude by certification level and motor class

### 1.1 Certification levels and impulse classes

- NAR Level 1 allows H and I motors. Some F and G motors can also need Level 1. [S2]
- NAR Level 2 allows J, K and L motors. [S2]
- NAR Level 3 allows M, N and O motors. [S2]
- A NAR Level 3 flight must use a motor with more than 5,120 N·s total impulse. [S3]
- The NAR HPR Safety Code gives these impulse bands: H ≤ 320 N·s; I 320.01–640; J 640.01–1,280; K 1,280.01–2,560; L 2,560.01–5,120; M 5,120.01–10,240; N 10,240.01–20,480; O 20,480.01–40,960 N·s. [S4, Minimum Distance Table]
- The NAR model rocket code limits a model rocket to 1,500 g, 125 g of propellant, and 320 N·s. [S5, item 7]
- Tripoli certification counts: not found.

## 3. Ceilings

### 3.1 FAA Part 101 (14 CFR 101 Subpart C) [S1]

- 14 CFR 101.22(a): Class 1 Model Rocket uses ≤125 g propellant and weighs ≤1,500 g.
- 14 CFR 101.22(b): Class 2 High-Power Rocket has a combined total impulse of ≤40,960 N·s (9,208 lbf·s).
- 14 CFR 101.22(c): Class 3 Advanced High-Power Rocket is any amateur rocket that is not Class 1 or Class 2.
- 14 CFR 101.25(b): no Class 2/3 operation where horizontal visibility is less than five miles.
- 14 CFR 101.25(e) and (f): FAA authorization is necessary within 5 NM of an airport and in controlled airspace.
- 14 CFR 101.25(g): separation from non-associated persons and property is the greater of one-quarter of the maximum expected altitude or 1,500 ft.
- 14 CFR 101.29(b)(2): a Class 3 waiver request must state the "maximum possible range".

## 4. Horizontal distance, drift and recovery distance

### 4.1 Rules and design limits

- NASA Student Launch: "The recovery area shall be limited to a 2,500 ft. radius from the launch pads." [S29, PDF p.17 req 3.10]
- NASA Student Launch: descent time is limited to 90 seconds (apogee to touchdown). [S29, PDF p.17 req 3.11]
- NASA Student Launch: teams calculate drift at 0, 5, 10, 15 and 20 mph wind, with apogee directly above the pad. [S29, PDF p.24]
- NASA Student Launch: trackers "shall not exceed 250 mW of power (per transmitter)". [S29, PDF p.17]
- NAR HPR code: the launcher points within 20 degrees of vertical. No launch if wind exceeds 20 mph. [S4]
- NAR HPR code: the site's smallest dimension is at least one-half of the maximum allowed altitude, or 1,500 ft, whichever is greater. [S4]
- LDRS 44: no launch more than 20 degrees from vertical; range shuts down above 20 mph wind. [S18]
- MDRA: flights over 2,000 ft must be dual deploy or chute release. [S23]
- IREC 2026 DTEG: main deploy at or below 457 m (1,500 ft) AGL. Landing descent rate below 11 m/s (36 ft/s). [S26, PDF p.23, 6.4.1 and 6.4.3]
- IREC 2026 DTEG: dual-event recovery is required above 457 m (1,500 ft) AGL apogee. [S26, PDF p.22, 6.1.1]

## 9. Computed from sourced inputs (slant range)

Formula: slant range S = sqrt(A² + H²).
- A = apogee AGL. H = horizontal distance from the ground station to the landing point (or the drift distance).
- This assumes the ground station is at the pad and the worst-case geometry is the rocket at apogee height above the landing point. This is a bounding assumption, not a sourced fact.
- Flat Earth. No terrain. No antenna pattern.

### 9.1 Inputs and how they map to segments (mapping is an assumption)

- "Typical" apogee for L1, L2 and L3 is a computed median. L1 = median of 29 LOC IV H/I RockSim values [S7]. L2 = median of 139 J/K/L RockSim values from the Level-2 and Katana kits [S8, S9]. L3 = median of 11 reported M flights: Iris RRC2 readings 8,876; 8,844; 9,663; 4,255; 9,730; 5,007; 6,733; 9,613 ft; Iris PerfectFlite 8,757 ft [S10]; LEM-IV 11,775 ft [S11]; Thunder Struck 13,654 ft [S12].
- Low-power H values are proxies. They use the NAR minimum site dimension (C 400 ft, E–G 1,000 ft, two Gs 1,500 ft) [S5]. Landing distance data for low power: not found.
- H = 300 ft is "about 100 yards" (Iris, L1120, 3,923 ft apogee) [S10].
- H = 2,500 ft is the NASA SL recovery radius [S29].
- H = 3,379 ft is "about .64 miles" (Eggfinder guide example) [S30].
- H = 3,500 ft is LEM-IV [S11]. H = 5,280 ft is "1+ miles" (Thunder Struck) [S12].
- H = 10,560 ft is the Eggtimer "about 2 miles downrange" statement [S30].
- H = 52,800 ft is the 10-mile distance to WSMR in the 2023 SA Cup DTEG [S27]. This is an outer bound, not a typical landing.
